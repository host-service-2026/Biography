From 1997 to 2026 Hazel Dooney was a fine artist, writer and strategist skilled in concept development, stakeholder communications, project management, literary essays, online communication simultaneously speaking to multiple audiences, digital strategy, the development and management of strategic networks, and management of intellectual property.

From 1997 to 2001 Dooney produced major artworks for six solo exhibitions. In 2001 she was the youngest and only female artist invited to an expedition to central Australia with well-known senior artists. In 2002, this resulted in the coffee table book _William Creek & Beyond_ and documentary _The View from Here_, aired on ABC TV.[1][2]

In 2003 Dooney commissioned the website hazeldooney.com.[3] In 2004 she produced major artworks for a final solo exhibition as a represented artist. In 2005 she restructured her business to establish a direct-to-consumer model for fine art by connecting directly with the audience for her work via the internet, initially via email and a private newsletter disseminated via word of mouth.

In July 2006 Dooney was featured on the front page of the _Business_ section of _The Age_ newspaper in an article titled ‘State-of-the-art selling rivals play to the galleries’.[4] She began her blog, ‘Self Vs Self’, the same month. In August her first essay, ‘Life Study’, was published by _Griffith Review_, with an extract published in _The Australian Financial Review_ in September.[5] Later the same month, a feature on Dooney was published in _The Courier Mail_’s magazine, _QWeekend_.[6] The tagline on the cover read, “Why painter Hazel Dooney is her own best agent.” 

The primary business model was to create new series of artworks, and produce new works for each series via private commission, which continued throughout her career. Dooney used online and email publishing as her marketing, alongside engagement with media until 2014. As the internet evolved to better host images, she expanded her marketing to include documentary-style photography.

In 2007 Dooney was included in _Vogue Australia_’s ‘Australia’s Most Wanted’, featuring cultural figures developing international success.[7] Two major artworks were sold by Christie’s London in 2007 for 10,000 GBP each (23,000 AUD approx.) and a third was sold in 2008 for 14,375 GBP (32,200 AUD approx.).[8][9][10]

In 2011 Dooney was included as a case study in the ‘Strategic Networks’ chapter of university textbook _Strategy: Theory and Practice_.[11]

Her essay ‘Broken’ was published in 2013 by _Griffith Review_ and included in _The Best Australian Essays 2013_, edited by Robert Manne.[12] The same year, after reading her blog, Lord Mayor Robert Doyle invited Dooney to create a public artwork for The City of Melbourne. The 15 metre text based mural was located on Royal Lane, off Bourke Street in Melbourne’s CBD, and remained beyond the initial agreement to 2019.[13]

From 2014 Dooney restructured to reduce her public profile, moving away from marketing via interviews to avoid media sensationalism. Instead, she expanded her own writing and direct-to-audience communications to increase asset scarcity and further tailor private stakeholder communications.

From 2018 to 2021 Dooney worked on an international private portrait commission and until late 2022 developed new work under the patronage of entrepreneur, investor and corporate advisor Mark Carnegie. 

From 2022 to 2024 she wrote a column for _Coagula Art Journal_.[14]

In 2023 Dooney’s essay ‘The future of art fraud’ was published by _Griffith Review_.[15] She also attended University of Sydney, earning a High Distinction (85) for FRNC1601: French 1 and a Distinction (82) for FRNC1602: French 2. 

In 2024 Dooney developed the concept and digital strategy for _everythingthe.com_, a direct-subscription publication for Australia's leading art critic, John McDonald.[16] 

At the end of the 2026 financial year, Hazel Dooney formally concluded her career as an artist and essayist, deleted all social media accounts and finalised all business operations in alignment with Australian Taxation Office requirements.
